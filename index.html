The big decision captured here

In our kick-off conversation on April 21, there was a tension between two ideas:

  • Oscar's view: ideally the form itself should filter applicants — block people from booking if they haven't proven eligibility, so staff only see qualified requests.
  • Joanne's view: don't block anyone from booking — many families are newcomers with language and tech barriers, and if they can't book online they will just walk into the depot, making things worse.

The model shown above is the middle path we landed on together: every family who submits the form gets a pending appointment slot, and Snowsuit Fund staff review and approve (or reject) before the appointment is confirmed. Nobody gets turned away at the form. The filter happens at step 8, done by your staff, the day before the appointment — not at the depot, and not automatically by the system.

This was Oscar's good question on metrics, and it shows up in the "request vs approval rate" figure in the reporting dashboard. Over time that number tells you how well the intake form is guiding families toward the right documents.

What's been carried through from your current process

  • 30-minute slots with 10 families per slot — same structure you run today on WordPress.
  • Suggested size by child's age (e.g. newborn → 6-month) with last-year's-size-plus-one for returning families.
  • Point-of-sale model at the depot — staff adjusts the actual size taken before the sale finalizes, inventory only drops when finalized.
  • $10 service fee per snowsuit, waivable for shelter families.
  • Exchange flow preserved for families who come back later for a different size.
  • Family ID and Order number carried over from the legacy VT220 system so returning families can still be looked up the same way.

What's new or different

  • One system, not three. Today, the booking, the paper referral form, and the Linux terminal are all disconnected. In Mareto, a family submits once and it flows through.
  • Document verification moves from the depot floor to staff desks. Your team reviews uploaded documents the day before the appointment, not while the family is standing in line.
  • Duplicate detection on submission or on staff approval — combinations of last name + date of birth, last name + postal code, or address.
  • Children's names collected on the form — we weren't collecting them before, but this helps clarify the form for parents (who sometimes enter their children's names in the parent fields) and improves duplicate detection.
  • Live dashboards replace the Excel worksheets Joanne builds by hand.
  • Separate boys' and girls' jacket inventory, per Joanne's request.

Questions for your team to think through

These are the spots where we need your direction before we start configuring. No right answers — just pick what feels right for how your team actually works. If you're unsure, flag it and we'll talk through it on the next call.

  1. Rejection path. When staff reject an application at step 8, should the family get an email explaining why and telling them what they'd need to resubmit — or just a generic "your application was not approved" message?
  2. Auto-approval for clear cases? If an Ontario Works letter is uploaded (which auto-qualifies), do you still want every application to go through staff review — or would you trust the system to auto-approve those and only route the trickier CRA Child Benefit cases to staff? Joanne, this is your call — safer to review all, faster to auto-approve the easy ones.
  3. CRA download helper. Families often struggle to find their Canada Child Benefit notice in the CRA My Account portal. Do you want us to embed a short step-by-step guide (in English and French) right next to the upload field, so families can walk through it at home? Possibly a short video, per Travis's suggestion.
  4. Referral agencies. When an agency submits on behalf of a family, should they use the same public form (tagged with agency name), or do they need their own login so they can see the status of the families they've referred?
  5. Children's names. Arda suggested collecting first names for each child (helps duplicate detection and clarifies the form for parents). Any concerns from Joanne or Cheryl on this?
  6. Exchange authority. When a family comes back to exchange a snowsuit, should every staff member be able to process it, or is that a more limited role? You mentioned Wendy tends to do exchanges today.
  7. What's on the day-of dashboard? When staff open Mareto first thing in the morning, what do they most need to see? A list of today's approved appointments, pending reviews, low-stock alerts, yesterday's distribution — which three would be most useful at a glance?
  8. Languages on the front-facing form. Bilingual English / French is confirmed. Cheryl mentioned Arabic being common at the depot. Do you want Arabic as a third form option at launch, or leave it for phase 2 once the core is running?
  9. Postal code boundary. Do you want the form to warn families whose postal code is outside Ottawa (K1 range), or just accept and let staff sort it out during review?
  10. Blocking specific days. You mentioned wanting to block slots for holidays or vacation. Who should be able to do that — just Joanne, or any staff member?
